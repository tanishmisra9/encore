# Product

## Users

A family caregiver sitting beside a parent or partner with dementia, with a phone in hand, wanting something to do together that works. A hackathon judge arrives cold and needs to understand the idea and try it in under a minute.

## Purpose

Memory Lane builds a short reminiscence session from the years someone was young: songs, films and shows from roughly ages 10 to 30, in their own culture, found with Qloo from artists they loved. The caregiver plays it, records how each item landed, and the plan adapts for that person.

## Positioning

**The right song isn't the popular one. It's the one from when they were 19.**

## Boundaries

- An activity planner for caregivers. It is not therapy and gives no medical advice.
- No health data is asked or stored. Only a birth year, one to three artists, and an optional first name.
- No language model decides anything. Qloo finds candidates; plain code decides what is safe to play.
- A Qloo failure is shown as a failure. It never falls back to a generic list.
- Reactions apply only to the person in that session and can be deleted.

## Brand commitments

- Product name: Memory Lane.
- Direct, sparse copy with no em dashes. One idea per viewport.
- Square corners everywhere. No pills.
- A jukebox title-strip wall: warm black cabinet, off-white strips, stock-color code blocks.
- Never clinical, never sepia nostalgia, never a generic dark music grid, never an agent dashboard up front.
